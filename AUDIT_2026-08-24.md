# FractalMergeCritic audit — 2026-08-24

> This report freezes the evidence for the original isolated-damage critic. The sequential rate–distortion replacement described in `README.md` and `VALIDATION.md` was implemented later the same day. The red large-training verdict remains until the replacement clears a held-out pilot.

## Decision

**RED for large image or text training. GREEN only for the bounded redesign experiment described below.**

The implementation now executes the intended counterfactual: merge a legal token pair inside a frozen pretrained transformer, measure downstream damage against the unmerged teacher, and train a small critic and bounded refiner from those labels. Exact no-op and cache-sensitive tests pass on real CLIP and Qwen models. The present critic, however, does not beat cosine similarity on held-out images or text, and therefore has not justified a large corpus or larger backbone run.

## What is theoretically sound

For frozen transformer suffix `F_l`, hidden tokens `H_l`, legal merge `M_ij`, and task divergence `D`, the oracle label is

`Delta_l(i,j) = D(F_l(H_l), F_l(M_ij(H_l)))`.

Learning `q_theta(H_l, i, j) ~= Delta_l(i,j)` amortizes an otherwise expensive counterfactual. Mass-weighted means are associative and preserve first moments. Adding `log(mass)` to attention logits exactly restores multiplicity when the merged keys and values are identical, and is an approximation when they are merely similar.

The limitation is fundamental rather than an implementation detail: independently safe merges can interact, later merge rounds are out of the calibration distribution, and one-step next-token KL does not guarantee continuation quality. This is adaptive post-training token coarsening, not dataset-level BPE and not a universal tokenizer.

## Why large training is not approved

The required scientific test is not whether the critic learns a nonzero correlation. It must outperform the strongest nearly-free baseline at the actual selection budget. It presently loses to cosine at the important 10–25% vision budgets and at 25% for text. Scaling the same objective would be an expensive hope, not an evidence-backed experiment.

The refiner consistently helps, but its mean benefit is too small to rescue poor pair selection. Safety probabilities are not operationally calibrated, and no GPU end-to-end speedup has yet been measured.

## Approved next experiment

Run one bounded redesign on existing calibration corpora before generating more teacher labels:

1. Fit a per-layer monotonic cosine baseline and train the critic only on residual damage plus a pairwise ranking objective.
2. Include rollout states produced by simultaneous first-round merges and label second-round candidates there.
3. Calibrate safe probabilities on a separate grouped calibration split.
4. For images, add a dense DINO/segmentation preservation target, since global CLIP damage can miss local destruction.
5. For text, replace one-step KL with multi-token teacher-forced continuation KL plus a retrieval or long-context task metric.
6. Run the queued GPU benchmarks and require measured end-to-end prefill/vision speedup after critic overhead.

Only green-light a larger run if the critic beats cosine by at least `0.05` Spearman and lower regret at both 10% and 25% merge budgets, false-safe rate is at most 10% on nonempty selected sets, and end-to-end throughput improves. Evaluate repeated merge rounds, not just isolated pairs.
